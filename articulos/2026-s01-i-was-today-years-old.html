Cultura Semana 01

Internet & Everyday Wonder

I Was Today Years Old

Ten tiny collapses of certainty, collected from the corner of the internet where everyone finds out everything ten years late.

Consejo editorial · 8 de septiembre de 2026 · 6 min de lectura

There is a particular kind of vertigo that only the internet can produce. You open an app to waste four minutes, and you close it having learned that the tiny pocket inside your jeans pocket was designed to hold a pocket watch. Social media is regularly accused of shortening our attention, and it probably does — but along the way it also became the largest and strangest classroom ever built: one where a marine biologist, a retired locksmith and somebody’s grandmother all teach at the same time, unscheduled and unsupervised.

Out of that chaos came a hashtag that names the exact second the floor moves under you: #IWasTodayYearsOld. It marks the moment a fact you never thought to question quietly falls apart, and you look around to find that everyone else appears to have received the memo years ago. It is equal parts humiliation and delight — which is, more or less, the definition of learning.

For our first issue we went looking for those moments: the things we were completely certain about as children, and the afternoon we found out otherwise. Here are ten of them.

Why We Keep Falling For It

None of these facts are urgent. Nobody passes a course because of the pocket watch, and the moon will keep not following anybody. What the hashtag really documents is the small, public willingness to say I did not know that — a sentence that gets harder to pronounce with every year of school we complete.

The internet did not make us more ignorant. It made our ignorance visible, searchable, and occasionally very funny.

So this is our opening invitation for the semester: bring us the moment the floor moved under you. We are collecting them.
